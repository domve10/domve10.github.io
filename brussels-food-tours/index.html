Brussels Food Tours & Chocolate Workshops: A Chef's Guide (2026)

I cook professionally in Brussels. When friends visit, this is what I tell them to eat, and the kind of guided tasting I tell them to book if they only have a day or two.

What to eat in Brussels (and what to skip)

  • Frites: twice-fried in beef fat at a proper friterie, in a paper cone, with one sauce. Andalouse or mayonnaise, not ketchup.
  • Waffles: the Liège waffle (dense, caramelised pearl sugar) is the street one; the Brussels waffle (light, rectangular) is the sit-down one. Skip the waffles buried under toppings near the Grand-Place.
  • Pralines: the Belgian filled chocolate. Taste a plain dark praline first; that's where you see the quality of the couverture.
  • Moules-frites: best from autumn to early spring, when Zeeland mussels are in season.
  • Carbonade flamande, stoemp, shrimp croquettes: the real Belgian home cooking, more interesting than the tourist menus.
  • Beer: Trappist, gueuze and lambic. Lambic is spontaneously fermented, which is basically wild fermentation in a barrel.

Food tours: is a guided tasting worth it?

If you have less than two days, yes. A walking food tour gets you to five or six good places in three hours, skips the tourist traps around the Grand-Place, and the price usually covers all the tastings. Look for small groups, a clear list of included tastings and recent reviews.

Chocolate workshops

A chocolate workshop is the best rainy-day activity in Brussels, and good for kids. In a decent one you temper chocolate, fill your own pralines and take them home. As a chef, the part I'd pay for is tempering: once you understand the three temperatures, you can do it in any kitchen.

Beer tastings in Brussels and Bruges

A guided tasting is the easiest way to understand the difference between a Trappist, a gueuze and a kriek without drinking ten full glasses. Bruges is one hour by train and makes a good day trip.

A chef's tip

Eat frites standing up, straight away. After five minutes in the cone they steam and go soft, and no tour can fix that.

FAQ

What food is Brussels famous for?

Frites, Liège and Brussels waffles, pralines and chocolate, moules-frites, carbonade flamande, shrimp croquettes and Belgian beer such as Trappist, gueuze and lambic.

How long is a typical Brussels food tour?

Most walking food tours last about 2.5 to 4 hours. Check the booking page for the exact duration and the tastings included.

Are chocolate workshops good for children?

Many are family friendly, but age limits vary by workshop, so check the listing before booking.
