Cake & Bake Pricing Calculator for Home Bakers (with Labour)

Pricing a celebration cake, a tray of focaccia or a private dinner by gut feeling usually means working for free. This calculator uses the formula from the Chef's Pricing Kit: ingredients + packaging + labour + overhead = True Cost, then a price at the margin you choose.

The True Price formula

True Cost = (ingredients + packaging + labour) × (1 + overhead %)
Labour = minutes ÷ 60 × hourly rate
Price (ex-VAT) = True Cost ÷ (1 − margin %)

Worked example from the free cheat sheet: one butter croissant with €0.40 of ingredients, €0.10 of packaging and 7.5 minutes of labour at €25/h has a True Cost of €4.17, and a price of €6.42 at 35% margin. Many home bakers sell it at €2.00.

The six mistakes this fixes

  1. Ignoring labour: active baking time is money.
  2. Using only food cost %: 30% looks healthy until a three-hour cake pays €8 an hour.
  3. Forgetting packaging and delivery.
  4. Copying a neighbour's price: their rent, speed and taxes are not yours.
  5. Never updating costs when butter and eggs move.
  6. Rounding down to "sound nice".

FAQ

How much should I charge for a homemade cake?

Add ingredients, packaging, your hands-on time at an hourly rate and an overhead share to get the True Cost, then divide by (1 − your margin). A cake with €14.50 ingredients, €3.20 packaging and 4 hours at €25/h has a True Cost of about €135 before margin.

What hourly rate should a home baker use?

Use what you would accept for skilled work in your area. The calculator defaults to €25/h as an example; replace it with your own.

Is this tax advice?

No. VAT and tax rules depend on your country and status. Set VAT to 0 if you are not registered and check your own situation.
