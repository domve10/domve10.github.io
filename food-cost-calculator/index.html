Food Cost Calculator: Recipe & Plate Cost per Portion (Free)

Cost a recipe the way a professional kitchen does: from the price you actually pay for each pack, the amount the recipe uses, and the yield left after trimming and peeling. You get the batch cost, the cost per portion and a menu price at your target food cost %.

IngredientPack pricePack sizeUsed in recipeYield %Cost

Pack size and "used" must be in the same unit (g, ml or pieces). Yield % = usable part after trimming (e.g. onions ≈ 90%, whole fish ≈ 45–50%).

How the calculator works

  1. Cost per unit = pack price ÷ (pack size × yield %).
  2. Ingredient cost = cost per unit × quantity used.
  3. Portion cost = sum of all ingredient costs ÷ portions.
  4. Menu price (before VAT) = portion cost ÷ target food cost %.

Example: a 5 kg bag of potatoes at €6 with 85% yield after peeling costs €6 ÷ (5000 g × 0.85) = €0.0014 per usable gram. 800 g in a recipe = €1.13.

Why yield matters

Most home sheets divide the pack price by the pack weight and stop there. In a kitchen you pay for the peel, bones and trim too. A salmon side bought at €22/kg with 80% usable fillet really costs €27.50 per usable kilo. Ignore yield and every dish looks 10–20% cheaper than it is.

Food cost % is not the whole price

Food cost % tells you nothing about labour. A dish that takes 40 minutes of hands-on work can show a "healthy" 28% food cost and still lose money. If you sell cakes, catering or private dinners, use the True Cost calculator, which adds labour, packaging and overhead.

FAQ

What is a good food cost percentage?

Many restaurants work with roughly 25–35% food cost per dish, but the right number depends on your labour, rent and style of service. A labour-heavy dish needs a lower food cost %, a simple one can carry a higher one.

How do I calculate food cost per portion?

Add up the cost of every ingredient the recipe uses (pack price divided by usable pack size, times quantity used), then divide by the number of portions the batch makes.

What is yield percentage?

The usable part of an ingredient after trimming, peeling, boning or cooking loss. If 1 kg of whole carrots gives 850 g peeled, the yield is 85%.

How do I turn food cost into a menu price?

Divide the portion cost by your target food cost %. A €4.20 portion at 30% food cost gives €14.00 before VAT.
