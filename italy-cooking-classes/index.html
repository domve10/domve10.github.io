Cooking Classes in Italy: Where to Learn Pasta, Pizza and Regional Food (2026 Chef's Guide)

I'm an Italian professional chef. A cooking class is the best souvenir you can bring home from Italy, but each city teaches something different. This is what I'd learn where.

How to choose a good class

  • Small groups (around 12 people or fewer), so you actually touch the dough.
  • You make the dish yourself from scratch, not watch a demo.
  • You eat what you made, ideally with local wine.
  • The menu is regional. Fettuccine Alfredo in Bologna is a red flag.

Bologna: fresh egg pasta

Bologna is the home of the sfoglia, egg pasta rolled by hand with a long pin. Learn tagliatelle, tortellini and the real ragù alla bolognese: beef, soffritto, a little tomato, milk and wine, and two or three hours of slow cooking.

Rome: carbonara, cacio e pepe and amatriciana

Roman pasta is about technique, not ingredients. Carbonara is guanciale, egg yolks, Pecorino Romano and black pepper, and no cream. The trick a good class teaches is the emulsion with the pasta water, off the heat.

Florence: Tuscan cooking and market tours

Florence is good for classes that start with a market visit (Mercato Centrale or Sant'Ambrogio) and end with pici, ribollita or a tiramisù. Pick one with the market included.

See cooking classes in Florence

Naples: pizza

In Naples, learn pizza from a pizzaiolo: dough hydration, stretching by hand without a rolling pin, and why a 450 °C oven matters. At home you can get close with a steel and the grill on full.

A chef's tip

Write down the flour, the egg ratio and the dough weight on the day. Most people forget the numbers by the time they're home, and the numbers are the class.

FAQ

Which Italian city is best for a cooking class?

It depends on what you want to learn: Bologna for fresh egg pasta and ragù, Rome for carbonara and cacio e pepe, Florence for Tuscan cooking with a market tour, Naples for pizza.

Is cream used in authentic carbonara?

No. Authentic Roman carbonara uses guanciale, egg yolks (or eggs), Pecorino Romano and black pepper; the creaminess comes from emulsifying with pasta water.

How long is a typical cooking class in Italy?

Most last about 3 to 4 hours including the meal. Check the booking page for exact times.
