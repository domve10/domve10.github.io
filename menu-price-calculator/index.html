The formula

Menu price before VAT = portion cost ÷ target food cost %. Then add VAT and round up. Rounding €14.70 down to €14 looks friendlier but quietly removes 5% of your margin on every plate.

Which target should I use?

  • Lower food cost % (25–28%): dishes with a lot of hands-on prep, cheap base ingredients (pasta, risotto, soups, desserts).
  • Middle (30–33%): most mains.
  • Higher (35–40%): premium proteins and wine, where a fixed cash margin matters more than the percentage.

The default 12% VAT is only an example (it is a common Belgian restaurant rate for food). Always use your own rate.

FAQ

How do you calculate a menu price?

Divide the cost of one portion by the food cost percentage you want, then add VAT. A €4.20 plate at 30% is €14.00 before VAT.

Should I round menu prices up or down?

Up. Rounding down on every dish lowers your margin across the whole menu; round up to a clean price you can defend.

Does food cost % include labour?

No. Food cost % only covers ingredients. For cakes, catering and private dinners, include labour with a True Cost calculation.
