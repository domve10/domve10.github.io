Chef notes: what not to scale straight
  • Salt and spices: scale to about 80% when you more than double, then taste.
  • Liquids for risotto, braises and stocks: a wider pan evaporates faster; keep extra hot stock ready.
  • Raising agents (baking powder, yeast) in very large batches: scale slightly under and allow more time.
  • Eggs: round to whole eggs, or weigh them (1 medium egg ≈ 50 g without shell).
  • Cooking time does not scale with quantity: a bigger cake in a bigger pan needs a lower temperature and a longer bake; check the core.


Pan-size maths
Round pans scale by area: a 24 cm pan holds (24 ÷ 20)² = 1.44× the batter of a 20 cm pan. Rectangular pans: multiply width × length.

FAQ
How do I scale a recipe up?
Divide the new number of servings by the original to get a factor, then multiply every ingredient by it. Taste salt and spices rather than scaling them fully.
How do I convert a recipe to a bigger round cake pan?
Square the ratio of the diameters. From 20 cm to 24 cm: (24/20)² = 1.44, so make 1.44 times the batter.
Does cooking time change when I double a recipe?
Usually yes, but not proportionally. Larger volumes take longer; check doneness by core temperature or a skewer instead of the clock.
